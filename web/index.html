Private messaging without a phone number.

Fog is a messenger with the Signal protocol's end-to-end encryption. You sign up with a key instead of your identity, and our server can't read your messages or tell who sent them.

Open Fog in your browser Free. No email, no phone, no ads.

Or get the app for iPhone, Android, macOS, Windows and Linux.

Your account is a key, not your identity.

When you create an account, Fog makes 24 words on your device. They are your account: they derive your encryption key, and your account ID is its public half. Pick a username if you like, so friends can find you. There's nothing to link back to you.

Because your ID is your key, nobody can quietly swap it out. To be sure a username belongs to who you think, scan their safety code in the chat.

Everything you'd expect from a messenger.

Privacy shouldn't mean giving things up. All of it is end-to-end encrypted.

Photos, video and any file
Up to 2 GB each, from holiday videos to source code. Encrypted on your device before upload.
Calls, voice and video messages
Calls with screen sharing, and voice notes or short videos in a tap. Relaying keeps your IP address from the person you're calling.
Groups
The server can't tell a group from ordinary one-to-one chats. Changes are signed by the group's owner.
On all your devices
Link your computer with a QR code: your chats come along, and messages, calls and reads stay in step everywhere.
Disappearing messages
From 30 seconds to a week, set per chat. Replies, reactions, edits, pins, polls and stickers work as you'd expect.
Message requests
Strangers land in a separate inbox. They can't see read receipts, and their calls don't ring, until you accept.

How your messages are protected.

The Signal protocol, unmodified
Fog uses Signal's own open-source library, with post-quantum key agreement (PQXDH with Kyber) and the Double Ratchet. Every message gets a fresh key.
Sealed sender
Once you've accepted someone, their messages to you arrive without a sender attached. Only your device learns who wrote them.
Padding
Messages are padded to 160-byte steps and attachments to size buckets, so sizes give little away.
Verification you can do in seconds
Scan each other's safety code once, and Fog marks the chat verified. Keys can't change behind your back.
Locked on your device
App lock encrypts your keys and chats at rest, unlocked by a passcode (Argon2id) or Touch ID, Face ID or Windows Hello.
No third parties
No analytics, no ad networks, no Google fonts or STUN servers. Calls connect through our own servers or directly.

What our server can and can't see.

A messenger has to move your messages somehow. Here's exactly what that costs you with Fog.

It can't see

  • What you write, send or say on a call
  • Your photos, videos, files and voice messages
  • The sender’s account ID on sealed-sender messages
  • Which groups you're in, or who's in them
  • Your name and profile photo
  • Your phone number or email from signup

It can see

  • Your IP address when you connect. Use a VPN or Tor if that matters to you.
  • The username you choose, if you choose one
  • The sender’s account and device IDs on ordinary deliveries, including first contacts
  • When you're connected, and roughly how much you send

Don't trust us. Check.

Fog is open source under the AGPL, client and server alike. The web app builds byte-for-byte reproducibly, so you can confirm getfog.org serves exactly the published code.

Or run your own: one command brings up the server, HTTPS and a call relay on your domain.

View the source on GitHub 
# run your own Fog (set your domain and a secret in .env)
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
# check what getfog.org serves
bun web/verify.ts https://getfog.org ./my-build
All 17 files match.

Questions

Can I use Fog on more than one device? +

Yes. Link up to four more devices, in a browser, the desktop app or a phone app, by scanning a QR code from the device that has your recovery phrase. Each new device gets your chat history and its own encryption keys, and keeps working when the others are offline. You can unlink any of them at any time.

What happens if I lose my recovery phrase? +

The account can't be recovered. There's no email or phone number to reset it with, which is also why nobody else can take it over. Write the words down somewhere safe.

Is a web app safe for private messaging? +

A web app is only as trustworthy as the server that delivers it. Fog's builds are reproducible so anyone can check them, and the desktop app ships the code once instead of loading it on every visit.

How is this different from Signal? +

Fog uses the same encryption library. The differences are around it: no phone number at all, accounts tied to a key you hold, and a server you can run yourself.

Who pays for it? +

Fog has no ads and collects nothing to sell. The server is small and cheap to run, and anyone can host their own.
